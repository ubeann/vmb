# Nx Cloud vs Turborepo for this repo

Researched on 2026-10-02. Question: the owner's priority is performance and efficient resource use (CI minutes, cache hits, local dev speed, cost). Should this repo replace **Turborepo** with **Nx** on **Nx Cloud's free tier**? How does that compare with keeping Turborepo, or with Vite+'s task runner?

Primary sources only: nx.dev, the nrwl/nx repo, turborepo.dev, vercel.com docs, docs.github.com, developers.cloudflare.com, npm registry metadata, and this repo's own files and CI history (`gh`, read-only). A claim that a source doesn't settle is marked **unverified**. This builds on [Vite+ and Oxlint](vite-plus-and-oxlint.md) and does not repeat it.

## TL;DR

- **Do not move to Nx Cloud's free tier. It cannot carry this repo's CI volume.** Hobby gives 50,000 credits a month, and every CI pipeline execution (one workflow run) costs 500 credits ([pricing](https://nx.dev/pricing), [credits](https://nx.dev/docs/reference/nx-cloud/credits-pricing)). That covers about **100 runs**. This repo ran about **1,090 Nx-relevant workflow runs per 30 days** (ci.yml plus preview builds), so the allowance would run out in about **3 days**. After that, "Nx Cloud features turn off … and turn back on at the next reset" ([CI features](https://nx.dev/docs/features/ci-features)), which removes the remote cache that ADR-0023's docs-only PR gate relies on. The paid Team plan would cost roughly **$300/month** at this volume.
- **The task runner is not where the CI minutes go.** Turborepo-run steps account for about **380 raw minutes of an estimated ~4,050 billed minutes per 30 days (~9%)**, already at a **75% task cache-hit rate**. The misses are real changes, mostly in `apps/web`, and no runner can skip those. Swapping in Nx saves about **0** minutes.
- **The Turborepo setup works.** The Vercel remote cache has been live since 2026-09-17: `TURBO_TOKEN`/`TURBO_TEAM` are set, and logs show "Remote caching enabled" on 319 of 345 Verify jobs. The cache is free on every Vercel plan, subject to fair use ([Vercel](https://vercel.com/docs/monorepos/remote-caching)). `Release` replayed its build from cache 70 out of 70 times.
- **The real savings are per-job minute rounding and one uncached job.** GitHub bills every job rounded up to a whole minute ([runner pricing](https://docs.github.com/en/billing/reference/actions-runner-pricing)). Raw job time was 1,137 minutes but billed time was 2,069, an 82% rounding overhead. `Real Postgres` re-runs the db suite on every push without a cache (~22% of billed minutes), and 14-second `Lint Workflows and Scripts` jobs bill 1 minute each (~14%). Fixing those two would save an estimated **~1,000 minutes per 30 days**. A runner swap saves ~0.
- **The pace is close to the GitHub Free quota.** By my estimate (the billing API is not readable with this token), CI billed **~1,898 minutes in September**, in a month where CI only started on 09-12. That pace projects to **~4,050 per 30 days**, against an allowance of 2,000. Without a payment method, "usage is blocked once you use up your quota" ([GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/billing/concepts/product-billing/github-actions)).
- **Recommendation: (a) keep Turborepo and the Vercel remote cache, and fix the two CI gaps.** No ADR superseding 0008 is needed. Nx with a self-hosted cache and Vite+ both cost more effort for no measured gain. Revisit only if the team grows past 5 contributors, or if CI becomes long enough for distributed execution to pay.

- **Per month:** September (09-12 to 09-30) came to ~1,898 billed minutes. From 09-17, when the remote cache went live, to 10-02 (15.2 days), the total was 2,056 billed minutes, which projects to **~4,050 per 30 days**. Run rate per 30 days: ci.yml ~705 runs (about 220 of them master pushes), preview.yml ~480, cleanup.yml ~250. Activity is bursty (57 CI runs on 09-17, 2 on 09-26).
- **Billed minutes by job (since 09-17):** Verify 26%, Real Postgres 22%, preview Create Environment 16%, Lint Workflows and Scripts 14%, preview Detect Code-Relevant Changes 9%, cleanup 6%, Release 4%. Verify is the only job where the task runner does meaningful work.
- **Verify job (since 09-24, 129 successful runs):** setup (mise, pnpm store, install) takes 21 s median and Format 7 s. The `turbo build test lint typecheck` step takes 1 s on a full cache hit and 57 s median on a miss. The whole job takes 36 s on a full hit and 95 s on a miss. 45% of runs were full hits, and the billed-minute split was 66 jobs at 1 minute, 59 at 2, and 15 at 3 or more.
- **Turbo cache hit rates from the "Cached: X cached, Y total" lines:**
  - Verify with the remote cache: **80% of tasks** (2,796 of 3,514), with 177 of 292 runs at FULL TURBO. Since 09-24: 75% (1,169 of 1,548).
  - Misses since 09-24 concentrate in `@vmb/web` (build 76 of 135, test 71 of 129, lint 76, typecheck 71). `@vmb/api`, `@vmb/db` and `@vmb/types` tasks hit 85–91% of the time.
  - `format:check`: 33 hits, 104 misses. Expected, because its inputs are the whole tree.
  - `Release` build: 100% (70 of 70). Preview web build: 17% (43 of 247), because `PUBLIC_API_URL` is per PR and most pushes change `apps/web`.
- **Remote cache host:** the Vercel Remote Cache. "Remote caching enabled" first appears on 2026-09-17 (branch `chore/ci/turbo-remote-cache`) and is present on every same-repo run after that. The `Detect turbo remote-cache credentials` step writes to `$GITHUB_OUTPUT` without echoing, but the `actions/cache` fallback ran only on Dependabot runs. So **both secrets are set** for normal runs and empty for Dependabot runs, as ADR-0020 intends.
- **The Dependabot fallback cache yielded 0 hits in 10 runs.** It restored a `turbo-local-` entry each time and still re-ran every task. A dependency bump changes the resolved dependencies that turbo hashes, and the entry is only ever saved by earlier Dependabot runs. So the fallback can rarely hit (inference from the measurements). It costs about 1 s, so this is harmless but low-value.
- **Real Postgres** (`pnpm --filter @vmb/db test`, outside turbo) takes 94 s median: 25 s for containers, 20 s for setup, 41 s for the suite. That is always 2 billed minutes, even though the same `@vmb/db:test` hits the cache ~88% of the time in Verify.

## Findings

### Nx Cloud free tier (pricing page read 2026-10-02; page dated 2026-09-30)

- **Plan:** "Hobby", "Free forever", "50,000 credits (resets monthly)", "5 complimentary contributors", "10 concurrent CI connections", "All Nx Agents resource types", no credit card ([pricing](https://nx.dev/pricing), [Nx Cloud](https://nx.dev/nx-cloud)). A contributor is "any person or actor who authored the commit of a CI Pipeline Execution" in the cycle ([pricing](https://nx.dev/pricing)).
- **What consumes credits:** a CI Pipeline Execution costs 500 credits. Agents cost per minute (Linux AMD64: small 5, medium 10, large 20, XL 40). Self-healing CI costs 6,555 credits per 1M input tokens and 32,700 per 1M output tokens. A resource usage report costs 10 ([credits](https://nx.dev/docs/reference/nx-cloud/credits-pricing)). "A CI Pipeline Execution is a CI run or a Workflow run … running a PR or running CI on the main branch are CI Pipeline Executions" ([credits](https://nx.dev/docs/reference/nx-cloud/credits-pricing)). The page does not carve out cache-only runs. Whether local developer cache use costs credits is **unverified**; the page doesn't say.
- **At the limit:** "On the Hobby plan, Nx Cloud features turn off once the monthly credits are gone and turn back on at the next reset". Unused credits don't carry over ([CI features](https://nx.dev/docs/features/ci-features)).
- **Next tier:** Team costs $29/month and includes $29 of usage. Beyond that it is $5.50 per 10,000 credits and $19 per contributor ([pricing](https://nx.dev/pricing)).
- **Cache retention:** **unverified**. Neither the pricing page nor the remote-cache docs state it.
- **Fit to this repo:** ~705 ci.yml runs plus ~385 preview runs that would call Nx makes ~1,090 executions, or ~545,000 credits per 30 days. That is about 11 times the Hobby allowance. On Team, the cost would be roughly 492,000 extra credits × $5.50 per 10,000 + $29 ≈ **$300/month**, before any agent minutes. By comparison, paying GitHub for every minute over the quota at $0.006 per Linux minute ([GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/billing/concepts/product-billing/github-actions)) would cost about **$12/month** at the projected ~4,050 minutes.
- **Secret-free runs:** unauthenticated access through `nxCloudId` defaults to read-write ("ID Access Level … By default this is set to read-write") ([cache security](https://nx.dev/docs/concepts/ci-concepts/cache-security)). Dependabot and fork runs could therefore reach the cache without secrets, but each would still consume a pipeline execution. The same page advises read-only tokens on unprotected branches.

### Self-hosted caching for Nx

- In 2025 Nx made self-hosted caching free. It announced first-party bucket plugins and an OpenAPI spec for custom servers ("Nx remains free for everyone who chooses to self-host their own cache") ([blog, 2025-03-31](https://nx.dev/blog/custom-runners-and-self-hosted-caching)). Powerpack stopped being a standalone product on 2025-12-10 ([search result summarising nx.dev Powerpack docs](https://21.nx.dev/docs/enterprise/powerpack/licenses-and-trials); not read directly, so **unverified**).
- **On 2026-05-21 those bucket plugins were deprecated.** `@nx/s3-cache`, `@nx/gcs-cache`, `@nx/azure-cache` and `@nx/shared-fs-cache` were deprecated because of CVE-2025-36852 ("CREEP"): "the flaw is in their design and cannot be patched". They "will not receive updates or security patches" ([deprecation notice](https://nx.dev/docs/reference/deprecated/self-hosted-cache-packages)). On npm they now show `license: "Commercial"` and a deprecation message (`@nx/s3-cache@5.0.7`, 2026-05-22) ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/@nx/s3-cache)).
- **What remains is a custom server.** It must implement the Nx remote-cache OpenAPI spec (`PUT`/`GET /v1/cache/{hash}`, `NX_SELF_HOSTED_REMOTE_CACHE_SERVER`) and must return 409 rather than overwrite an existing key ([self-hosted caching](https://nx.dev/docs/kb/self-hosted-caching)). There is no first-party R2 or Workers implementation. Nx says "Most teams should use Nx Cloud instead" ([deprecation notice](https://nx.dev/docs/reference/deprecated/self-hosted-cache-packages)). A GitHub Actions cache backend for Nx is not documented. Persisting the local cache directory with `actions/cache`, as this repo already does for turbo, is the only route, and it shares the 0-hit problem above.

### Turborepo remote cache today

- "Vercel Remote Cache is free to use on all plans, even if you do not host your applications on Vercel" ([turborepo.dev](https://turborepo.dev/docs/core-concepts/remote-caching)). Fair-use limits on Hobby are 100 GB/month upload and 100 artifact requests per minute. Artifacts expire after 7 days ([Vercel, updated 2026-08-13](https://vercel.com/docs/monorepos/remote-caching)). This repo sends about 13 task lookups per run, far below both limits.
- OIDC is now supported and recommended over a long-lived PAT: `vercel/setup-turborepo-remote-cache-action` mints a short-lived, cache-only token ([external CI/CD](https://vercel.com/docs/monorepos/remote-caching/external-ci-cd)). Today the repo uses `TURBO_TOKEN` as a secret.
- **Self-hosting:** an open OpenAPI spec exists ("all versions of `turbo` are compatible with the `v8` endpoints"), along with optional HMAC artifact signing ([turborepo.dev](https://turborepo.dev/docs/core-concepts/remote-caching)). Listed community servers include `ducktors/turborepo-remote-cache` (MIT, v2.14.1 on 2026-09-17) and `brunojppb/turbo-cache-server`. `AdiRishi/turborepo-remote-cache-cloudflare` (MIT, v4.0.0 on 2026-01-19) runs on Workers with R2 or KV and has cron-based expiry ([repo](https://github.com/AdiRishi/turborepo-remote-cache-cloudflare)). R2's free tier is 10 GB-month, 1M Class A and 10M Class B operations, with free egress ([R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)). At this repo's volume, that is $0.

### Performance features

- **Affected:** Nx's guide replaces `turbo run` with `nx affected -t …` ([from Turborepo](https://nx.dev/docs/kb/from-turborepo)). Turborepo has `--affected`, equivalent to `--filter=...[main...HEAD]`, overridable with `TURBO_SCM_BASE`/`TURBO_SCM_HEAD`, and on a shallow checkout "all packages will be considered changed" ([run reference](https://turborepo.dev/docs/reference/run)). **Neither matters here.** ADR-0023 forbids path-scoping the gate, and unaffected tasks already replay from cache in about 1 s.
- **Nx Agents (distributed execution) and flaky-task retry:** Hobby includes agents, but they bill per minute on top of the 500-credit execution ([credits](https://nx.dev/docs/reference/nx-cloud/credits-pricing)). Automatic flaky-task retry needs distributed execution ("send that task to a different agent … up to 2 tries") ([flaky tasks](https://canary.nx.dev/docs/features/ci-features/flaky-tasks)). With a 57 s miss-path task run, splitting work across agents cannot shorten GitHub's 1-minute billing floor. It would only add credits.
- **Self-healing CI** needs an Nx Cloud VCS integration and spends token-priced credits ([self-healing](https://nx.dev/docs/features/ci-features/self-healing-ci), [credits](https://nx.dev/docs/reference/nx-cloud/credits-pricing)). Whether it is on by default on Hobby is **unverified**.
- **Task inference:** `@nx/vite` infers `build`/`dev`/`preview`/`typecheck` from `vite.config.*` ([Vite plugin](https://nx.dev/docs/technologies/build-tools/vite/introduction)). `@nx/vite`, `@nx/vitest`, `@nx/eslint` and `@nx/playwright` are all at 23.2.1 under MIT ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/@nx/vite)). There is **no official SvelteKit or Cloudflare plugin**: `@nx/sveltekit` and `@nx/cloudflare` return 404 on npm. The community options are `@nxext/sveltekit` 23.0.0 (2026-07-09) and `@naxodev/nx-cloudflare` 7.1.0. `nx init` keeps package.json scripts ("Your package.json scripts stay as they are") ([from Turborepo](https://nx.dev/docs/kb/from-turborepo)), so `svelte-check`, `wrangler` and the explicit scripts would work as they do today. Whether `@nx/vite` inference gets SvelteKit's `.svelte-kit/` outputs right is **unverified**.
- **Migration:** `npx nx@latest init` "detects `turbo.json`, writes an equivalent `nx.json`". Task `env` moves to `inputs: [{ "env": … }]`, `persistent` becomes `continuous`, and "Do not add `passThroughEnv` equivalents, because Nx does not block environment variables at runtime" ([from Turborepo](https://nx.dev/docs/kb/from-turborepo)).
- **Local dev:** Turborepo's daemon "is no longer used for `turbo run`"; it remains only for `turbo watch` and the LSP ([run reference](https://turborepo.dev/docs/reference/run)). Nx's daemon is on locally and off in CI. In Nx's own large repo it fell from 1.5–1.6 GB to about 200 MB in 22.7 ([22.7 release, 2026-04-27](https://nx.dev/blog/nx-22-7-release)). An open issue reports the daemon "burns 100-230% CPU on macOS … not backported to 23.2.x" ([nrwl/nx#37201](https://github.com/nrwl/nx/issues/37201), opened 2026-09-26). Both tools share a cache across git worktrees: turbo through this repo's unset `cacheDir` (comment in `ci.yml`), and Nx from 22.7.
- **Toolchain fit:** Nx 23 needs Node 22 or later ([Nx 23, 2026-06-17](https://nx.dev/blog/nx-23-release)). The repo is on 26. There is no official pnpm 12 statement from Nx (**unverified**). Open pnpm-related issues include lockfile pruning ([#37226](https://github.com/nrwl/nx/issues/37226)) and a graph that silently drops TypeScript import edges when `typescript` isn't resolvable from `nx` ([#37244](https://github.com/nrwl/nx/issues/37244)). A pnpm-12 bug in `nx release publish` was fixed ([#36860](https://github.com/nrwl/nx/issues/36860)). Turborepo is measured working here on pnpm 12.4.1. mise is irrelevant to either tool, since both install as devDependencies.

### Overhead

- **Install weight** (npm metadata, darwin-arm64, ranges resolved crudely, ignoring dedupe with existing deps): `turbo@2.11.6` is 2 packages and ~34 MB, with no dependencies beyond one platform binary ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/turbo)). `nx@23.2.1` is ~129 packages and ~57 MB, with 124 direct dependencies including `axios`, `yargs` and `ora` plus a ~19 MB native binary ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/nx)).
- **Rust core:** still yes for Nx. `packages/nx/Cargo.toml` exists and `nx` ships `@nx/nx-<platform>` native binaries as optional deps, though the repo is mostly TypeScript by bytes ([nrwl/nx](https://github.com/nrwl/nx)). Turborepo is mostly Rust.
- **Telemetry:** turbo collects anonymous usage by default. Opt out with `turbo telemetry disable` or `TURBO_TELEMETRY_DISABLED=1` ([telemetry](https://turborepo.dev/docs/telemetry)). Nx asks on first interactive run, never sends from CI unless `analytics: true`, and can be disabled with `"analytics": false` ([search summary of nx.dev telemetry](https://nx.dev/docs/reference/telemetry); page not read directly).

### Vite+ (from the prior note)

Vite Task has no remote cache yet ("planned for future versions"), and its GitHub Actions cache reuse is experimental. Replacing Turborepo would regress ADR-0023's docs-only replay. See [Vite+ and Oxlint](vite-plus-and-oxlint.md#recommendation).

## Gaps found in the current setup

1. **`Real Postgres` never caches.** `pnpm --filter @vmb/db test` bypasses turbo. Running `pnpm turbo test --filter=@vmb/db` with `TEST_DATABASE_URL` (already in `turbo.json`'s `test.env`, and a constant string, so it gives a stable key) would replay on the ~88% of runs where `@vmb/db` is unchanged. The job would drop from about 94 s (2 min) to about 55 s (1 min), saving an estimated **~400 billed min per 30 days**. One catch: the `postgres:18` image tag is not in the hash, so a server minor bump alone would not re-run the suite. Adding the image tag to `env` or `inputs` would close that.
2. **Per-job rounding on tiny jobs.** `Lint Workflows and Scripts` runs 14 s and bills 1 min; about 650 such jobs run per 30 days. Folding it into `Verify` as named steps costs only a few seconds there, because `Verify`'s setup already installs the full mise toolchain, including actionlint and shellcheck. That would save an estimated **~600 min per 30 days**. It reverses the separate-job choice from #61/#79, so it needs a decision. ADR-0023 already applied this "named step, not a job" reasoning to `Format`. The 6 s preview `Detect Code-Relevant Changes` job (~360 min per 30 days) is the same pattern but harder to fold, because it gates whether the preview job runs at all.
3. **Cache-poisoning (CREEP) exposure.** Same-repo PR runs hold a read-write `TURBO_TOKEN`, and `Release` deploys build output replayed from the remote cache (70 of 70). Turbo supports `--cache=local:rw,remote:r` for PR runs ([run reference](https://turborepo.dev/docs/reference/run)). The cost is that master pushes stop replaying entries their PR wrote, about +1 min on each of ~220 master pushes per 30 days. On a private, solo repo where forks get no secrets (ADR-0030), the risk is low. Record it rather than act on it now.
4. **The Dependabot `actions/cache` fallback** yielded 0 of 10 hits. It is harmless, and it is the only path that gives secret-free runs a cache. Leave it, or drop it for simplicity.

## Recommendation

1. **Keep Turborepo with the Vercel remote cache (option a). ADR-0008 stands.** Its trade-off still holds, and the measurements back it: a ~75–80% task hit rate, 1 s cached replays, a 100% cached `Release` build, $0 cost, and a single ~34 MB binary dependency.
2. **Spend the effort on the CI minutes the task runner doesn't touch.** Route `Real Postgres` through turbo's cache (gap 1). Decide whether to fold `Lint Workflows and Scripts` into `Verify` (gap 2). Together these are an estimated ~1,000 billed minutes per 30 days, enough to bring the projected pace back near the 2,000-minute quota.
3. **Do not adopt Nx Cloud on Hobby.** Its credit model charges per CI run, and this repo runs CI far more often than 100 times a month. Nx's real strengths, Agents and flaky retry, pay off on long multi-agent pipelines, not on a 57 s critical path.
4. **Keep option (b) in reserve.** If Vercel changes its terms or the 7-day expiry starts costing hits on long-lived PRs, a Workers and R2 cache is a drop-in swap of env vars, with no runner change.
5. **Revisit Nx** only if contributors exceed 5, CI critical paths grow to many minutes, or Nx changes how it prices per-run executions.

## Open questions for you

- **Billing:** can you check actual Actions usage? Either `gh auth refresh -s user` so `gh api /users/ubeann/settings/billing/usage` works, or open github.com/settings/billing. Then confirm whether September stayed under 2,000 minutes and whether a payment method or budget is set. If not, CI stops when the quota runs out.
- **Gap 2:** is folding `Lint Workflows and Scripts` into `Verify` acceptable, given the #61/#79 reasoning for a separate job?
- **Gap 1:** should replaying `Real Postgres` from cache be allowed? It is the check that catches embedded-versus-real Postgres disagreement (issue #109). Caching it means an unchanged `@vmb/db` is not re-checked against a new `postgres:18` minor unless the image tag goes into the hash.
- **Vercel plan:** is the Vercel team on Hobby? That sets the fair-use limits (100 artifact requests per minute), which this repo is far below today.
- **OIDC:** do you want to move `TURBO_TOKEN` to Vercel OIDC? Whether Dependabot `pull_request` runs can mint the `id-token` is **unverified**. If they can, it would give them a remote cache without secrets.
